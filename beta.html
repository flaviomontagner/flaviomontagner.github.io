Beta Platform

Case study · Flavio Montagner

My role Research, UX design and implementation, end to end: I shadowed the validation team, designed the workflows and built the platform.

10:00<2:00

minutes to turn one tester report into a Jira ticket. 60–70 reports a day.

with Beta Platform, the workspace I researched, designed and built for a validation team serving about 2,000 testers across 14 car brands.

Scroll to run the clock

Before: one report, four hand-offs.

  1. Find it.

    Read the report in a separate tool, then look up its campaign and build.

  2. Copy it.

    Open a Jira draft and paste in the description, screenshots and logs.

  3. Reconcile it.

    Spot drafts about the same issue, merge them, and check Jira for updates.

  4. Report it.

    Export tickets and campaign KPIs and assemble the weekly update by hand.

One dot, one report. An average day: 60–70. A peak day: more than 120.

+

Each one took 10 minutes to become a ticket. The weekly report took about 90 more.

Management asked for a faster workflow. Before drawing anything, I interviewed the validation team and sat beside them as they worked through the queue, from the incoming report to the weekly update their leads relied on.

The ten minutes weren’t spent writing. They went on looking things up: which build, which logs, and whether Jira already had the issue.

Three decisions took the copying out.

⤢123

A campaign gets one page.

Everything a reviewer used to look up now sits on the campaign itself.

  1. 1Feedback received, who responded and the response rate, for this campaign only.
  2. 2Build, app version and target OS next to the feedback, so nobody has to look them up.
  3. 3What testers were asked to verify travels with the campaign.
⤢1234

The draft arrives already written.

The team reviews a ticket instead of typing one.

  1. 1Two reports, one Jira issue. The originals stay separate in the platform.
  2. 2Summary, priority and severity arrive prefilled and stay editable.
  3. 3The description is assembled from the source reports, with the provider ticket and reporter.
  4. 4Before sending, the footer says exactly what Jira will receive and which links are kept.

Existing Jira links decide what happens next.

Nobody has to remember whether an issue already exists. Beta reads the links on the selected reports and picks the route. Nothing in Jira gets overwritten.

Reviewed draft

  1. The first report about a problem

    Create the Jira issue

  2. More reports about the same problem

    Add them to the open issue

    Beta rebuilds the Jira description from every linked report and brings in their logs and screenshots. One issue, all the evidence.

  3. Reports tied to several issues, or to one Beta can’t edit

    Open a new issue that points to the old ones

    The earlier issues stay exactly as they are and get linked to the new one.

Synced from Jira

  • Open
  • On hold
  • Rejected
  • Released

After sending, every report shows the live status and priority of its Jira issue, so the team doesn’t switch tools to check. If Jira refuses an attachment or a link, Beta says so and the team can try again.

Ten part-time days to a working MVP.

≈10 days part-time

Once the team and I had agreed how the workflow should run, I coordinated access to the internal and third-party APIs and built the first version with AI coding tools.

2 weeks with the team

Ten rounds of shadowing and interviews with five team members, watching where they hesitated or needed more context. More than twenty changes came out of it.

Built with DesAI Thinking

I observed and interviewed the team first, then let AI build the first version from the workflow we had defined. My time went into judging it with the people who would use it: ten rounds of shadowing and more than twenty changes. AI did the volume; the decisions stayed with the validation.

Journey and service blueprint

Seven steps of a campaign: how much manual work each took before and after, what the team sees, what Beta does behind the screen and who is involved.

Manual effortBeforeWith Beta

HighMediumLow

Before

Open the campaign brief and check the separate tools for updates.
Check the build, dates and tester list separately.
Look up the person in the tester list and compare it with the report.
Open each report in the feedback tool and look up the build or related issues by hand.
Copy the report and its files into Jira, then reconcile the drafts and existing links by hand.
Switch between Jira and the feedback tool to check status and match it back to the report.
Export tickets and KPIs, reconcile the numbers and rebuild the weekly presentation.

With Beta Platform

See active campaigns and when feedback last refreshed from the platform overview.
Choose the build, dates and tester groups in one form. Check who is eligible.
Find a tester, add or edit their profile, or match an unfamiliar address to someone already registered.
Filter the queue, open the reports and compare descriptions, screenshots and logs before deciding what to group.
Review prefilled fields and evidence. Create a ticket, update one Beta can edit, or create a new ticket that keeps earlier links.
See the latest synced status and priority on the report. Open the linked issue in Jira for comments or status changes.
Filter campaign trends and participation, then open the reports behind a count in the same view.
